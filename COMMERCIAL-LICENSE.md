## 1. Do you need a commercial license?

**No, if you:** run PayPhone unmodified (or with your modifications published
under the AGPL) to accept payments for your own business; study it; fork it and
open-source the fork; integrate against its HTTP API or the SDKs.

**Yes, if you want to do any of the following without releasing your source
code under the AGPL:**

| Scenario | Why AGPL blocks it |
|---|---|
| Modified hosted service, closed source | AGPL §13 requires offering your full source to network users |
| White-label / rebrand and sell PayPhone hosting | Same — plus conveyance obligations on distribution |
| Embed the engine in a proprietary product, appliance or SaaS | Copyleft would force the whole product open |
| Internal compliance forbids copyleft dependencies | Common in banks, public companies, government vendors |
| You want legal indemnification, SLA and support | Not provided by the AGPL |

## 2. What a commercial license grants

- A perpetual license to the PayPhone codebase **as of your purchase date**,
  with updates included for the duration of an active subscription.
- **Full exemption from AGPL obligations** for your modifications and
  integrations — keep them closed, no source-disclosure duty.
- Trademark usage rights for "PayPhone™" under the white-label tier.
- Support and (at Scale/OEM) a service-level agreement.

It does **not** grant: the right to sublicense the open-source portions as if
they were yours, patent trolling rights, or resale of the engine itself.

## 5. Why dual licensing keeps PayPhone alive

The AGPL guarantees the network core stays open and free forever — nobody can
close a fork and nobody can lock merchants in. The commercial license funds
development by the people and companies that profit from PayPhone at a scale
where openness alone doesn't pay the bills. Contributions to the open project
are what make both sides possible — see [CONTRIBUTING.md](./CONTRIBUTING.md).
